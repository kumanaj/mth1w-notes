# Reflecting

> 📘 **Curriculum:** A1 · 📖 **Textbook:** §1.7, pp. 34–36 · [⬅ Strand A: Mathematical Thinking and Making Connections](../)

## Big idea
Keep checking your thinking *while* you solve and *after* you finish. Reflecting is what
turns a wrong answer into a right one.

## Key concepts
Ask yourself:
- Is my strategy the best one? Is there a more efficient one?
- Am I making errors along the way?
- Have I considered other strategies or tools?
- **Does my answer make sense?** (estimate first)
- Can I **verify** the solution, e.g. by substituting back in or solving a second way?

Also:
- Write down your strategy in words or symbols so you can evaluate it later.
- If the answer is wrong, re-check each step **or** try a different strategy.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Two strategies for the same problem
*How many numbers from 1 to 100 are divisible by 7?*
- Strategy A (list): 7, 14, 21, …, 98 → count them: 14.
- Strategy B (divide): $100 \div 7 \approx 14.3$ → 14 whole multiples.

Strategy B is faster; Strategy A is a good check.

### Example 2: Work backward, then verify
*Half of a number, decreased by $\frac{3}{4}$, gives $\frac{7}{12}$.*
$$\frac{n}{2} - \frac{3}{4} = \frac{7}{12} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{n}{2} = \frac{7}{12} + \frac{9}{12} = \frac{16}{12} = \frac{4}{3} \;\Rightarrow\; n = \frac{8}{3}$$
Verify: $\frac{8}{3} \div 2 = \frac{4}{3} = \frac{16}{12}$, and $\frac{16}{12} - \frac{9}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$ ✓

### Example 3: Does it make sense? (spotting errors)
A recipe needs 1 kg flour, 500 mL milk, 2 eggs, 125 g sugar. Karen tripled it to
3 kg flour, **15 L milk**, 6 eggs, **3.75 kg sugar**.
- 15 L of milk is far too much: $3 \times 500 \text{ mL} = 1500 \text{ mL} = 1.5 \text{ L}$.
- 3.75 kg of sugar is also too much: $3 \times 125 \text{ g} = 375 \text{ g}$.

She slipped a decimal place when converting units. Estimating would have caught it.

## Watch out
- ⚠️ Systematic trial needs a *system*. Change your guess based on what the last guess told you.
- ⚠️ A total of 19.38 dollars for one coffee and one muffin should make you stop and check!

## Quick check
1. What is the least number divisible by all of the integers 1 through 9?
2. Find five odd numbers that add up to 55. (Is there more than one answer?)

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Use the highest powers of the primes needed: $2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5 \times 7 = 2520$.
2. One answer is $7 + 9 + 11 + 13 + 15 = 55$. There are others, e.g. $1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 39$.

</details>
