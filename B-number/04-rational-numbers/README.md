# Rational Numbers

> 📘 **Curriculum:** B3.1, B3.2, B3.3, B3.4 · 📖 **Textbook:** §1.5 pp. 23–28 and Get Ready pp. 4–5, 102–103 · [⬅ Strand B: Number](../)

## Big idea
Integers and fractions are both **rational numbers**. Every operation with signed fractions uses
two skills together: **fraction rules** for the size and **integer rules** for the sign.

## Vocabulary
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **integer** | a positive or negative whole number, or zero: …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, … |
| **rational number** | a number that can be written as $\frac{a}{b}$, where $a, b$ are integers and $b \ne 0$, e.g. $-\frac{3}{5}$, $0.25$, $1\frac{3}{4}$, $-3$ |
| **unit fraction** | a fraction with numerator 1, e.g. $\frac{1}{4}$ |
| **reciprocal** | the number you multiply by to get 1; the reciprocal of $\frac{6}{7}$ is $\frac{7}{6}$ |

## Key concepts

### Integers describe location, direction and change (B3.1)
| Context | Positive | Negative |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Elevation / depth | above sea level | below sea level |
| Money | gain, deposit | loss, withdrawal |
| Temperature | above 0 °C | below 0 °C |
| Movement on a line | right / up | left / down |

**Integer rules**
- Add: $-2 + 5 = 3$, $\;2 + (-6) = -4$. Opposites add to zero: $4 + (-4) = 0$.
- Subtract = **add the opposite**: $4 - 9 = 4 + (-9) = -5$, $\;2 - (-4) = 2 + 4 = 6$
- Multiply/divide: **same signs → positive**, **different signs → negative**.
  $-9 \times (-5) = 45$, $\;20 \div (-4) = -5$

### Unit fractions build other fractions (B3.2)
$\frac{3}{4}$ is three copies of the unit fraction $\frac{1}{4}$. Measuring tools use this:
a ruler marked in $\frac{1}{16}$ inches, or a $\frac{1}{4}$-cup measure used 3 times for $\frac{3}{4}$ cup.

### Where the negative sign goes (B3.3)
$$-\frac{3}{4} = \frac{-3}{4} = \frac{3}{-4} = -0.75 \qquad \frac{-3}{-4} = \frac{3}{4}$$
One negative sign makes the fraction (or ratio, rate, decimal) negative; two cancel out.

### Operations with signed fractions (B3.4)
| Operation | Rule | Example |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Add/subtract | common denominator, then add numerators with integer rules | $\frac{3}{8} + \left(-\frac{1}{8}\right) = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4}$ |
| Multiply | change mixed numbers to improper fractions, cancel, multiply across | $\left(-\frac{3}{4}\right) \times \frac{1}{5} = -\frac{3}{20}$ |
| Divide | multiply by the reciprocal | $2\frac{1}{3} \div \frac{6}{7} = \frac{7}{3} \times \frac{7}{6} = \frac{49}{18} = 2\frac{13}{18}$ |

**Order of operations: BEDMAS.** **B**rackets → **E**xponents → **D**ivision/**M**ultiplication (left to right) → **A**ddition/**S**ubtraction (left to right).

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Add and subtract rational numbers
$$\frac{1}{2} + \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{6} + \frac{4}{6} = \frac{7}{6} = 1\frac{1}{6}$$

$$\frac{3}{5} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{5} + \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = \frac{12}{20} + \left(-\frac{5}{20}\right) = \frac{7}{20}$$

$$-1\frac{1}{2} - \left(-\frac{2}{3}\right) = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{2}{3} = -\frac{9}{6} + \frac{4}{6} = -\frac{5}{6}$$

### Example 2: Multiply and divide
$$\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right) \div \left(-\frac{4}{5}\right) = \left(-\frac{2}{3}\right) \times \left(-\frac{5}{4}\right) = \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}$$

### Example 3: Order of operations with integers
$$
\begin{aligned}
-2(15-18) + 7 - 3(4^2+10) &= -2(-3) + 7 - 3(16+10)\\
&= 6 + 7 - 78\\
&= -65
\end{aligned}
$$

### Example 4: In context
A business lost 6200 dollars in year 1 and 2150 dollars in year 2, then earned 4780 dollars in year 3.
$-6200 + (-2150) + 4780 = -3570$, so overall it **lost 3570 dollars**.

## Watch out
- ⚠️ $-\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6}$ is **more negative** than either fraction: $-\frac{5}{12}$, not $-\frac{1}{12}$.
- ⚠️ Don't round decimals in the middle of a calculation. Use fractions or keep all the digits.
- ⚠️ Division by zero is **undefined**.

## Quick check
1. $\frac{1}{2} + \left(-\frac{3}{4}\right)$
2. $-\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6}$
3. $\frac{5}{6} \times \left(-\frac{3}{10}\right)$
4. $\left(-\frac{7}{8}\right) \div \frac{5}{6}$
5. A diver is at −32 m and rises 9 m. Where is the diver now?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\frac{2}{4} - \frac{3}{4} = -\frac{1}{4}$
2. $-\frac{3}{12} - \frac{2}{12} = -\frac{5}{12}$
3. $-\frac{15}{60} = -\frac{1}{4}$
4. $-\frac{7}{8} \times \frac{6}{5} = -\frac{42}{40} = -\frac{21}{20} = -1\frac{1}{20}$
5. $-32 + 9 = -23$, so 23 m below the surface.

</details>
