# 3.3 Discover the Exponent Laws

> 📖 Textbook pp. 119–129 · [⬅ Chapter 3](../)

## Big idea
When powers have the **same base**, you can combine them by working with the exponents
instead of multiplying everything out.

## Key concepts
| Law | Rule | Why it works |
|-----|------|--------------|
| **Product rule** | $x^a \times x^b = x^{a+b}$ | $3^2 \times 3^4 = (3 \cdot 3)(3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3) = 3^6$ |
| **Quotient rule** | $x^a \div x^b = x^{a-b}$ | $\dfrac{5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5}{5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5} = 5^2$ (common factors cancel) |
| **Power of a power** | $(x^a)^b = x^{a \times b}$ | $(2^2)^3 = 2^2 \cdot 2^2 \cdot 2^2 = 2^6$ |

- A base with no exponent written has exponent **1**: $5 = 5^1$.
- **Zero exponent:** anything (except 0) divided by itself is 1, and the quotient rule gives $x^{a-a} = x^0$, so $x^0 = 1$.
- The laws work only for the **same base**. $3^4 \times 4^3$ can't be combined.
- With coefficients: multiply/divide the **numbers**, and apply the laws to the **variables**.
- A power outside brackets applies to **every factor** inside: $(4uv^2)^2 = 4^2 u^2 v^4 = 16u^2v^4$.
- Metric units are powers of 10: $1 \text{ km} = 10^3 \text{ m}$, $1 \text{ m} = 10^2 \text{ cm}$, so $1 \text{ km} = 10^3 \times 10^2 = 10^5 \text{ cm}$.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Product rule
$$3^2 \times 3^3 = 3^{5} = 243 \qquad 5^2 \times 5 \times 5^2 = 5^{2+1+2} = 5^5 = 3125$$
$$(-2)^4 \times (-2)^3 = (-2)^7 = -128 \qquad \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^3\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^5 = \tfrac{1}{32}$$
$$0.1^4 \times 0.1^2 = 0.1^6 = 0.000\,001$$

### Example 2: Quotient rule
$$8^7 \div 8^5 = 8^2 = 64 \qquad 4^7 \div 4 \div 4^3 = 4^{7-1-3} = 4^3 = 64$$
$$\frac{0.5^6}{0.5^3} = 0.5^3 = 0.125 \qquad \frac{\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^3\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2}{\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^5} = \left(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)^0 = 1$$

### Example 3: Power of a power
$$(3^2)^4 = 3^8 = 6561 \qquad [(-2)^3]^4 = (-2)^{12} = 4096$$
$$\left[\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^2\right]^2 = \left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^4 = \tfrac{16}{81} \qquad (0.2^3)^2 = 0.2^6 = 0.000\,064$$

### Example 4: Simplify algebraic expressions
a) $y^3 \times y^5 = y^8$

b) $6p^7 \div 3p^3 = (6 \div 3)\,p^{7-3} = 2p^4$

c) $a^2b^3 \times a^6b = a^{2+6}\,b^{3+1} = a^8b^4$

d) Simplify the top and bottom first, then divide:
$$\frac{(2uv^3)(8u^2v^2)}{(4uv^2)^2} = \frac{16u^3v^5}{16u^2v^4} = uv$$

Simplifying first makes substitution much easier: if $u = 3, v = 2$, the answer is just $3 \times 2 = 6$.

### Example 5: Scientific notation (extension)
$$(3 \times 10^5)(2 \times 10^4) = 6 \times 10^9 \qquad \frac{9 \times 10^8}{3 \times 10^5} = 3 \times 10^3$$

## Watch out
- ⚠️ $x^3 \times x^5 = x^8$, **not** $x^{15}$. Multiply powers → **add** exponents.
- ⚠️ $(x^3)^5 = x^{15}$. A power of a power → **multiply** exponents.
- ⚠️ $3^2 \times 3^3 \ne 9^5$. The base stays the same.
- ⚠️ $(2uv^2)^3 = 8u^3v^6$. Don't forget to cube the coefficient.

## Quick check
Simplify.
1. $m^8 \div m^5$
2. $(c^3)^4$
3. $(2uv^2)^3$
4. $12k^2m^8 \div 4km^5$
5. $(-6)^5 \div (-6)^2 \div (-6)^2$

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. $m^3$
2. $c^{12}$
3. $8u^3v^6$
4. $3km^3$
5. $(-6)^1 = -6$

</details>
