# 1.2 Focus on Communicating

> 📖 Textbook pp. 10–13 · [⬅ Chapter 1](../)

## Big idea
A correct answer only counts if someone else can follow it. Communicate with **words,
diagrams and symbols**, using correct mathematical form.

## Key concepts
- Use **math vocabulary** when explaining strategies (e.g. *prime*, *perfect square*, *hypotenuse*).
- Use **correct mathematical form**:
  - one `=` per line, lined up down the page
  - write a leading zero on decimals (`0.08`, not `.08`)
  - don't write things that aren't equal as equal
- Draw **neat, fully labelled diagrams**.
- Number systems are a form of communication. We use the **Hindu-Arabic** numerals 0–9 with
  place value; ancient Egyptians used separate symbols for 1, 10, 100 and 1000 and repeated them.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Use a diagram to communicate (Pythagorean relationship)
Squares are drawn on each side of a right triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5.

- Areas of the two smaller squares: $3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25$
- Area of the square on the hypotenuse: $5^2 = 25$

So the area of the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the areas on the other two sides:
$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

### Example 2: Fixing mathematical form
Greg wrote: `8% × 120 = .08 × 120 = 9.6`

Better:
$$
\begin{aligned}
8\% \text{ of } 120 &= 0.08 \times 120\\
&= 9.6
\end{aligned}
$$
(Write "of" or convert to a decimal first, use a leading zero, and put one step per line.)

### Example 3: Describe a sequence
$13, 15, 18, 22, \ldots$ The differences are $+2, +3, +4$, so next are $+5, +6$ → **27, 33**.

## Watch out
- ⚠️ "Running" equal signs like `5 + 3 = 8 × 2 = 16` are wrong, because $5 + 3 \ne 16$.
- ⚠️ A diagram without labels or units doesn't communicate much.

## Quick check
1. Describe the pattern and give the next two terms: $3, 6, 12, 24, \ldots$
2. Describe the pattern and give the next two terms: $100, 80, 65, 55, \ldots$
3. Use the rule "sum of the first $n$ odd numbers $= n^2$" to find $1 + 3 + 5 + \cdots + 99$.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Multiply by 2 → $48, 96$.
2. Subtract 20, 15, 10, then 5, 0 → $50, 50$.
3. 1 to 99 contains 50 odd numbers → $50^2 = 2500$.

</details>
