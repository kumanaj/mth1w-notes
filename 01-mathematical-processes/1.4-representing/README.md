# 1.4 Focus on Representing

> 📖 Textbook pp. 19–22 · [⬅ Chapter 1](../)

## Big idea
The same situation can be shown **numerically, graphically, algebraically, or with a
diagram**. Picking a good representation often makes the answer obvious.

## Vocabulary
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **vertex** (pl. *vertices*) | a point where two sides meet |

## Key concepts
- Drawing a **diagram** helps you see the situation.
- Representing data **numerically** (integers, tables) helps you spot patterns.
- Use **integers** for directions: up/down, gain/loss, north/south.
- On a **coordinate grid**, points are written $(x, y)$.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Scuba dive (integers + work backward)
Kevin dove down 6 m, came up 5 m, dove down 9 m, then down 2 m more, finishing at 32 m below the surface.
Where did he start?

Write the moves as integers: $-6, +5, -9, -2$. Net change: $-6 + 5 - 9 - 2 = -12$.

Work backward: $\text{start} = -32 - (-12) = -20$. **He entered the wreck at 20 m below the surface.**

### Example 2: Handshakes (diagram)
8 friends each shake hands once with each other. Place A–H in a circle and draw segments:
A has 7 new handshakes, B has 6 new, C has 5, …
$$7 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 28 \text{ handshakes}$$
(Same pattern as the chess problem in 1.1: $\frac{8 \times 7}{2} = 28$.)

### Example 3: Points on a grid
$A(2, 3), B(5, 4), C(8, 5)$: $x$ goes up 3 and $y$ goes up 1 each time.
Next three: $(11, 6), (14, 7), (17, 8)$.

## Watch out
- ⚠️ When working backward, **reverse the operations** (subtract a gain, add a loss).
- ⚠️ In $(x, y)$ the horizontal coordinate always comes first.

## Quick check
1. Treasure hunt: 2 km N, 5 km E, 4 km S, 3 km W, 2 km N. Where is the treasure relative to the start?
2. How many roads connect each pair of 6 towns directly?
3. A bike's driver cog has 30 teeth and the driving cog has 20. If the driver cog turns 3 times,
   how many turns does the driving cog make?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. North–south: $2 - 4 + 2 = 0$. East–west: $5 - 3 = 2$ → **2 km east** of the start.
2. $5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15$ roads.
3. $3 \times 30 = 90$ teeth pass, and $90 \div 20 = 4.5$ turns.

</details>
