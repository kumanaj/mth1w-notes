# 1.3 Focus on Connecting

> 📖 Textbook pp. 14–18 · [⬅ Chapter 1](../)

## Big idea
Real problems mix different kinds of math (number sense, geometry, measurement). **Connect**
math to daily life and connect the different areas of math to each other.

## Key concepts
- A **diagram** (not a picture!) shows only the important information, with labels.
- Many problems need skills from several areas, e.g. paint needed → area (measurement) +
  shapes (geometry) + rates (number sense).
- A **Fermi problem** uses estimation and reasonable assumptions to get an approximate answer
  (named after physicist Enrico Fermi). Break it into smaller questions you *can* estimate.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Bouncing ball (diagram)
A ball is dropped from 160 cm and bounces to 75% of its previous height each time.
Total distance travelled when it hits the ground for the **5th** time?

| Movement | Distance (cm) |
|----------|---------------|
| First drop | 160 |
| Up and down after bounce 1 | $2 \times 120 = 240$ |
| Up and down after bounce 2 | $2 \times 90 = 180$ |
| Up and down after bounce 3 | $2 \times 67.5 = 135$ |
| Up and down after bounce 4 | $2 \times 50.625 = 101.25$ |
| **Total** | **816.25 cm** |

### Example 2: Hockey point totals (organized list)
Win = 2, tie = 1, loss = 0. Possible point totals after 5 games?
Listing every (wins, ties, losses) combination shows totals from 0 up to $5 \times 2 = 10$,
and every value in between is possible → **11 point totals**.

### Example 3: Jelly beans in a 4-L jar (Fermi problem)
1. Treat a jelly bean as a cylinder: length ≈ 1.5 cm, radius ≈ 0.5 cm.
   $V = \pi r^2 h = \pi (0.5)^2(1.5) \approx 1.2 \text{ cm}^3$
2. Jar: $4 \text{ L} = 4000 \text{ cm}^3$
3. Assume about 10% is air: $0.9 \times 4000 = 3600 \text{ cm}^3$
4. $3600 \div 1.2 = 3000$

**About 3000 jelly beans.** Check: count how many fit in a 250-mL cup and multiply by 16.

## Watch out
- ⚠️ In Fermi problems, **state your assumptions**. Different assumptions can still give reasonable answers.
- ⚠️ Keep units consistent ($1 \text{ L} = 1000 \text{ cm}^3$).

## Quick check
1. A bird flies 800 m, turns and flies half as far back, turns and flies half as far again,
   for 6 flights total. Total distance flown?
2. A snail starts 20 m below ground in a pipe whose top is 7 m above ground. It climbs 4 m a day
   and slides back 3 m each night. How many days to reach the top?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. $800 + 400 + 200 + 100 + 50 + 25 = 1575$ m.
2. It has to climb 27 m. After day $n$'s climb it is at $(n-1) + 4$ m up. That reaches 27 when $n = 24$ → **24 days** (it doesn't slide back once it's at the top).

</details>
