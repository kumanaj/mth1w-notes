# Add and Subtract Polynomials

> 📘 **Curriculum:** C1.3, C1.4 · 📖 **Textbook:** §3.6, pp. 154–159 · [⬅ Strand C: Algebra](../)

## Big idea
- **Add:** remove the brackets and collect like terms.
- **Subtract:** add the **opposite**. Every sign in the second polynomial flips.

## Key concepts
- Adding: $(a + b) + (c + d) = a + b + c + d$. The brackets just disappear.
- The **opposite** of a polynomial changes the sign of *every* term:
  the opposite of $2y - 5$ is $-2y + 5$.
- Subtracting: $(a + b) - (c + d) = a + b - c - d$.
- With algebra tiles, subtracting = flip the colour of every tile in the second group, then add.
- Check by substituting a value (e.g. $x = 1$) into the original and the answer. They should match.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Add polynomials
$$
\begin{aligned}
(3x + 4) + (7x - 5) &= 3x + 4 + 7x - 5\\
&= 10x - 1
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x^2 - 3x + 1) + (x^2 + 5x - 6) &= 2x^2 + x^2 - 3x + 5x + 1 - 6\\
&= 3x^2 + 2x - 5
\end{aligned}
$$

### Example 2: Subtract polynomials
$$
\begin{aligned}
(4y + 7) - (2y - 5) &= 4y + 7 + (-2y + 5) && \text{add the opposite}\\
&= 4y + 7 - 2y + 5\\
&= 2y + 12
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5x^2 - 2x + 3) - (x^2 + 4x - 1) &= 5x^2 - 2x + 3 - x^2 - 4x + 1\\
&= 4x^2 - 6x + 4
\end{aligned}
$$

**Check** with $x = 1$: the left side is $(5 - 2 + 3) - (1 + 4 - 1) = 6 - 4 = 2$, and the right side is $4 - 6 + 4 = 2$ ✓

### Example 3: Perimeter
A triangle has sides $2x + 1$, $3x - 2$ and $x + 4$.
$P = (2x + 1) + (3x - 2) + (x + 4) = 6x + 3$.

## Watch out
- ⚠️ The most common mistake is changing only the **first** sign when subtracting:
  $(4y + 7) - (2y - 5) \ne 4y + 7 - 2y - 5$. **Both** signs flip, so it's $-2y + 5$.
- ⚠️ A plus sign in front of brackets changes nothing; a minus sign in front changes **everything** inside.

## Quick check
Simplify.
1. $(y - 2) + (3 - 6y)$
2. $(4m - 1) - (3m - 8)$
3. $(5 - 3d) - (d + 6)$
4. $(4k - 3) + (5 - k) - (5k + 3)$

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. $-5y + 1$
2. $m + 7$
3. $-4d - 1$
4. $-2k - 1$

</details>
