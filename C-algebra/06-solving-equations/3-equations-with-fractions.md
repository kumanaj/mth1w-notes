# Lesson 3: Equations With Fractions

> 📖 Textbook §4.3, pp. 204–210 · [⬅ Solving Equations](README.md)

## Big idea
Fractions are annoying, so **get rid of them first**. Multiply **every term on both sides** by the
**lowest common denominator (LCD)**. Then you have an ordinary equation like in Lesson 2.

## Finding the LCD
The LCD is the smallest number that every denominator divides into.

- **List multiples:** for $\frac{1}{6}$ and $\frac{1}{8}$: 6, 12, 18, **24** and 8, 16, **24** → LCD = 24
- **Quick tip:** if the denominators share no factors (like 3 and 5), just multiply them: $3 \times 5 = 15$.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: One fraction, so multiply by its denominator
$$
\begin{aligned}
6 &= \tfrac{1}{3}(8 + x) \\
3 \times 6 &= 3 \times \tfrac{1}{3}(8 + x) && \text{multiply both sides by 3}\\
18 &= 8 + x \\
10 &= x && \text{subtract 8}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3(y - 5)}{4} &= 7 \\
3(y - 5) &= 28 && \text{multiply both sides by 4}\\
3y - 15 &= 28 && \text{expand}\\
3y &= 43 && \text{add 15}\\
y &= \tfrac{43}{3} && \text{divide by 3 (a fraction answer is fine)}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Example 2: Two fractions, so multiply by the LCD
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{k - 2}{3} &= \frac{k + 4}{5} && \text{LCD of 3 and 5 is 15}\\
15 \times \frac{k - 2}{3} &= 15 \times \frac{k + 4}{5} && 15 \div 3 = 5, \;\; 15 \div 5 = 3\\
5(k - 2) &= 3(k + 4) \\
5k - 10 &= 3k + 12 && \text{expand}\\
2k &= 22 && \text{subtract } 3k\text{, add 10}\\
k &= 11
\end{aligned}
$$
Check: L.S. $= \frac{11 - 2}{3} = 3$. R.S. $= \frac{11 + 4}{5} = 3$ ✓

### Example 3: Fractions in front of brackets
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tfrac{1}{3}(2x + 5) &= \tfrac{3}{4}(x + 2) && \text{LCD of 3 and 4 is 12}\\
4(2x + 5) &= 9(x + 2) && 12 \times \tfrac{1}{3} = 4, \;\; 12 \times \tfrac{3}{4} = 9\\
8x + 20 &= 9x + 18 \\
2 &= x && \text{subtract } 8x \text{ and 18}
\end{aligned}
$$
Check: L.S. $= \frac{1}{3}(4 + 5) = 3$. R.S. $= \frac{3}{4}(2 + 2) = 3$ ✓

### Example 4: Don't forget the whole numbers!
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{2} - \frac{x}{5} &= 6 && \text{LCD is 10}\\
10 \cdot \frac{x}{2} - 10 \cdot \frac{x}{5} &= 10 \cdot 6 && \text{every term, including the 6!}\\
5x - 2x &= 60 \\
3x &= 60 \\
x &= 20
\end{aligned}
$$

### Example 5: In a formula (trapezoid garden)
A trapezoid garden has area $A = 50 \text{ m}^2$, front width $a = 8$ m and depth $h = 5$ m. Find the back width $b$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{(a + b)h}{2} \\
50 &= \frac{(8 + b)(5)}{2} && \text{substitute}\\
100 &= 5(8 + b) && \text{multiply by 2}\\
20 &= 8 + b && \text{divide by 5}\\
b &= 12
\end{aligned}
$$
**The back of the garden should be 12 m wide.**

## Watch out
- ⚠️ Multiply **every** term, including whole numbers and terms without fractions.
- ⚠️ After multiplying, keep the brackets: $5(k - 2)$, not $5k - 2$.
- ⚠️ $12 \times \frac{1}{3}(3y + 2) = 4(3y + 2)$, **not** $3y + 2$. The leftover factor 4 matters.

## Quick check
1. $\frac{x - 2}{3} = 5$
2. $\frac{2(h - 3)}{5} = 14$
3. $\frac{m - 3}{4} = \frac{m + 1}{3}$
4. $\frac{1}{4}(x + 3) = \frac{1}{3}(x - 2)$
5. By what number would you multiply both sides of $\frac{y - 2}{9} = \frac{y + 3}{3}$ to clear the fractions?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. $x - 2 = 15$, so $x = 17$
2. $2(h - 3) = 70 \Rightarrow 2h - 6 = 70 \Rightarrow h = 38$
3. $3(m - 3) = 4(m + 1) \Rightarrow 3m - 9 = 4m + 4 \Rightarrow m = -13$. Check: $\frac{-16}{4} = -4$ and $\frac{-12}{3} = -4$ ✓
4. $3(x + 3) = 4(x - 2) \Rightarrow 3x + 9 = 4x - 8 \Rightarrow x = 17$. Check: $\frac{20}{4} = 5$ and $\frac{15}{3} = 5$ ✓
5. **9**, because 3 already divides into 9.

</details>
