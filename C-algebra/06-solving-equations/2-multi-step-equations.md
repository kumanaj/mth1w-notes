# Lesson 2: Multi-Step Equations

> 📖 Textbook §4.2, pp. 196–203 · [⬅ Solving Equations](README.md)

## Big idea
When $x$ appears on **both sides**, gather all the **$x$-terms on one side** and all the
**numbers on the other**. If there are brackets, **expand them first**.

## Key concepts
- Moving a term across the equal sign really means doing the opposite operation to both sides.
  To get rid of $+2x$ on the right, subtract $2x$ from **both** sides.
- You can isolate the variable on **either side**. Choosing the side that keeps the variable's
  coefficient **positive** usually means fewer negatives to deal with.
- Brackets: use the distributive property. A minus sign in front of brackets flips every sign
  inside: $-(y - 2) = -y + 2$.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Variable on both sides
$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + 2 &= 2x - 4 \\
3x - 2x + 2 &= -4 && \text{subtract } 2x \text{ from both sides}\\
x + 2 &= -4 \\
x &= -4 - 2 && \text{subtract 2 from both sides}\\
x &= -6
\end{aligned}
$$
Check: L.S. $= 3(-6) + 2 = -16$. R.S. $= 2(-6) - 4 = -16$ ✓

### Example 2: Answer is a fraction
$$
\begin{aligned}
7 - 2k &= 8 - 5k \\
7 - 2k + 5k &= 8 && \text{add } 5k \text{ to both sides}\\
7 + 3k &= 8 \\
3k &= 1 && \text{subtract 7 from both sides}\\
k &= \tfrac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Example 3: Pick the easier side
Solve $5 - 3m = -2 - 2m$.

| Isolate on the left | Isolate on the right (shorter!) |
|---|---|
| $-3m + 2m = -2 - 5$ | $5 + 2 = -2m + 3m$ |
| $-m = -7$ | $7 = m$ |
| $m = 7$ | |

Both give $m = 7$. On the right, the coefficient came out positive, which saved a step.

### Example 4: Brackets
$$
\begin{aligned}
5(y - 3) - (y - 2) &= 19 \\
5y - 15 - y + 2 &= 19 && \text{expand; the minus flips both signs}\\
4y - 13 &= 19 && \text{collect like terms}\\
4y &= 32 && \text{add 13}\\
y &= 8 && \text{divide by 4}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Example 5: Brackets on both sides, with a check
$$
\begin{aligned}
2(x - 3) &= -3(x + 5) - 6 \\
2x - 6 &= -3x - 15 - 6 && \text{expand}\\
2x - 6 &= -3x - 21 \\
2x + 3x &= -21 + 6 && \text{add } 3x \text{ and add 6}\\
5x &= -15 \\
x &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

**Check** with $x = -3$. Use brackets when you substitute.

| L.S. | R.S. |
|------|------|
| $2[(-3) - 3]$ | $-3[(-3) + 5] - 6$ |
| $= 2(-6)$ | $= -3(2) - 6$ |
| $= -12$ | $= -12$ |

L.S. = R.S. ✓

### Example 6: Geometry
*In a triangle, the middle angle is double the smallest, and the largest is triple the smallest. Find all three angles.*

Let the smallest angle be $x$. Then the others are $2x$ and $3x$. The angles in a triangle add to 180°:
$$x + 2x + 3x = 180 \;\Rightarrow\; 6x = 180 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 30$$
**The angles are 30°, 60° and 90°.** Don't stop at $x$; answer the whole question!

> **Angle facts you'll need:** triangle angles add to **180°**, supplementary angles add to **180°**,
> complementary angles add to **90°**.

## Watch out
- ⚠️ $-(y - 2)$ is $-y + 2$, **not** $-y - 2$.
- ⚠️ Don't forget to multiply the **second** term inside the brackets: $5(y - 3) = 5y - 15$.
- ⚠️ Check in the **original** equation, not in one of your later lines (an earlier mistake would carry over).

## Quick check
1. $5x - 9 = 3x + 7$
2. $2(x + 2) = 4x - 2$
3. $4c - 3 = 3(c + 4)$
4. $3(p - 2) - (p + 4) = 0$
5. An angle is twice the size of its supplement. Find both angles.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. $2x = 16$, so $x = 8$
2. $2x + 4 = 4x - 2 \Rightarrow 6 = 2x \Rightarrow x = 3$
3. $4c - 3 = 3c + 12 \Rightarrow c = 15$
4. $3p - 6 - p - 4 = 0 \Rightarrow 2p - 10 = 0 \Rightarrow p = 5$
5. $x + 2x = 180 \Rightarrow x = 60$. **The angles are 60° and 120°.**

</details>
