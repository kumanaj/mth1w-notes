# Hypotheses and Sources of Data

> 📘 **Curriculum:** D2.2, D2.3 · 📖 **Textbook:** §2.1, pp. 42–47 · [⬅ Strand D: Data](../)

## Big idea
Start an investigation with a clear **hypothesis**, then decide whether to collect your own
data (**primary**) or use someone else's (**secondary**).

## Vocabulary
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **hypothesis** | a theory or statement that is either true or false; a statement that can be tested |
| **statistics** | numerical data, *or* the collection, organization and analysis of numerical data |
| **primary data** | original data that a researcher gathers specifically for a particular experiment or survey |
| **secondary data** | data that someone else has already gathered for some other purpose |

## Key concepts
- Every hypothesis is either **true or false**. If it is false, its **opposite** must be true.
- Hypotheses can involve numbers ("Most people's favourite number is 7") or not ("Boys and girls have different study habits").
- To write the opposite, negate the claim. Don't just flip it to the reverse extreme.

| | Primary data | Secondary data |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | your own survey, experiment, phone calls, checking store websites yourself | Statistics Canada, published reports, atlases, websites with someone else's survey results |
| ✅ Pros | exactly what you need; you control quality | fast, cheap, often huge sample sizes |
| ❌ Cons | time-consuming, smaller samples | may not fit your question; check who collected it and whether it's reliable or biased |

## Worked examples

### Example 1: State a hypothesis and its opposite
| Variables | Hypothesis | Opposite |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| driver's age & accident risk | As drivers age, their risk of an accident increases. | As drivers age, their risk does **not** increase (it decreases or stays the same). |
| attendance & marks | Marks decrease as students miss more classes. | Marks do **not** decrease as students miss more classes. |
| heights of boys & girls | On average, boys and girls of the same age are the same height. | On average, they are **not** the same height. |

### Example 2: Primary or secondary?
- Daniel phones 100 families about pets → **primary** (he did the survey).
- Cathy uses Statistics Canada car-ownership data → **secondary** (huge, reliable sample).
- Anja finds a teen-spending survey online → **secondary** (check the source; organizations sometimes publish only favourable results).
- Tomas checks 24 store websites for a DVD price → **primary** (he collected the prices himself, just using the Internet as a tool).

## Watch out
- ⚠️ Using the Internet doesn't automatically mean secondary data. Ask *who collected it, and for what purpose?*
- ⚠️ The opposite of "most people prefer X" is "most people do **not** prefer X", not "most people prefer Y".

## Quick check
1. State the opposite: "Chocolate is not the most popular flavour of ice cream."
2. Primary or secondary? An office manager hands out a questionnaire about summer hours.
3. Primary or secondary? A researcher downloads ride-length data from a transit authority's website.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Chocolate **is** the most popular flavour of ice cream.
2. Primary.
3. Secondary (the transit authority collected it for its own purposes).

</details>
